Chapter Six

When Love Feels One Sided

Recognizing effort, imbalance, and the needs beneath them.

There is a particular kind of loneliness that can exist inside a relationship, especially when you love someone but begin questioning whether the effort you are making is being returned.

You may spend time thinking about the other person, considering their needs, adjusting your schedule, making sacrifices, and looking for ways to make their life a little easier. You may remember the small things that matter to them, look forward to spending time together, and try to show affection in ways that communicate how much they mean to you.

Yet somewhere along the way, you begin wondering whether they are thinking about you in the same way.

Perhaps you are usually the one who initiates conversations, suggests activities, or tries to resolve disagreements. Maybe you are the person who notices when something is wrong and attempts to bring the relationship back to a better place. You may begin asking yourself whether the relationship would receive the same attention if you stopped making the effort.

That question can be difficult to admit because it carries the fear that the answer might confirm something you do not want to believe.

You may love someone deeply and still wonder whether you are receiving the same consideration in return.

But there is another side to this experience that deserves attention. Sometimes the person who feels they are giving everything may not recognize the ways the other person is trying to contribute. Sometimes both people are making an effort, but their efforts look different. And sometimes one person really is carrying more of the emotional responsibility than the other.

The challenge is learning how to recognize the difference.

When You Begin Keeping Track

It rarely starts with a decision to measure everything.

At first, you simply notice certain things. You remember the last time you planned something together, the last time they initiated a meaningful conversation, or the last time they asked how you were doing and genuinely stayed to hear the answer.

You remember the occasions when you adjusted your plans, made yourself available, or put aside something you wanted because you knew it mattered to them.

Then you begin noticing what does not happen.

You wonder why they do not think to do some of those things for you. You question whether they recognize the effort you make or whether they have simply become accustomed to receiving it. You may even begin holding back to see whether they will notice the difference.

This is when a relationship can quietly become a place of measurement.

One person begins thinking about everything they have done, while the other may have an entirely different understanding of how much they contribute. Each may believe they are giving more than they receive, and neither may fully understand why the other feels disappointed.

Keeping score rarely creates closeness. It often turns affection into an expectation that every action must be returned in equal measure.

However, there is an important distinction between keeping score and recognizing an unhealthy imbalance.

A relationship should not require two people to calculate every act of kindness, but it should allow both people to recognize when responsibility, attention, and emotional effort have become consistently unequal.

If one person is always initiating, always adjusting, always apologizing, and always trying to repair the relationship, that pattern deserves an honest conversation. Love cannot depend on one person continually carrying responsibilities that belong to both.

Giving Love and Receiving It Differently

People do not always express affection in the same way.

One person may show love by making plans and creating opportunities to spend time together. Another may demonstrate commitment by helping with practical responsibilities, providing support during difficult circumstances, or working toward financial stability.

Someone may value frequent communication, while their partner feels that being physically present and dependable communicates everything that needs to be said.

These differences can create a frustrating experience when neither person recognizes what the other is offering.

Imagine one person spending the evening hoping for an uninterrupted conversation. They want to hear about the other person’s day, share their own thoughts, and enjoy the feeling of being emotionally connected.

The other person has spent the day working, managing responsibilities, and arranging their schedule so they can be together. They believe that making time for the relationship is a meaningful expression of love.

When the evening arrives, one person wants conversation while the other wants to relax. The person seeking connection may interpret the lack of conversation as disinterest. The person who arranged the time together may feel that their effort is being overlooked.

Both may be disappointed because each is looking for something different from the same moment.

Neither person’s need is automatically unreasonable. The problem is that they may be measuring connection through different experiences.

This is why appreciation and communication must exist together. Recognizing what someone does should not require ignoring what you still need, and expressing what you need should not require dismissing everything the other person has already contributed.

When Effort Is Limited by Circumstances

There are seasons in life when people cannot offer everything they would like to give.

Work commitments, financial pressure, family responsibilities, health concerns, and legal restrictions can influence how much time and freedom someone has. A relationship may develop during circumstances that make ordinary experiences more difficult to arrange.

A couple may want to go somewhere spontaneously but find that planning is necessary. They may want greater privacy but currently have a living arrangement that does not provide it. They may be saving toward a home while navigating financial limitations that require patience and careful decisions.

In those circumstances, the amount of freedom a couple has may not accurately represent the amount of love or commitment between them.

Someone who cannot make spontaneous plans may still be making a genuine effort to create meaningful experiences. Someone who understands the limitations may still feel disappointed by the restrictions they place on the relationship.

Both experiences can be true.

It is important not to confuse someone’s limitations with a lack of interest, but it is equally important not to use those limitations to dismiss every unmet need.

Circumstances may explain why something cannot happen immediately. They do not eliminate the responsibility to communicate, show consideration, and make the most of the opportunities that are available.

A person may not be able to offer the freedom they wish they had, but they can still demonstrate thoughtfulness within the circumstances they face. The other person can recognize those efforts while remaining honest about what they hope will change.

A shared future is built through present decisions, not simply through promises about what life will eventually become.

When Appreciation Becomes Difficult

Feeling unappreciated can change the way someone experiences even the smallest interactions.

A kind gesture may no longer feel meaningful because the person has been waiting for something different. An attempt to help may be received with uncertainty because the person does not know whether the help is being offered freely or because it is expected to be acknowledged.

Sometimes someone genuinely wants to appreciate what their partner does but has become so focused on what is missing that they struggle to recognize what is already there.

Other times, a person has been expressing gratitude for years while their own needs remain consistently overlooked.

Both situations deserve honest examination.

Appreciation should not be used to silence someone who is asking for more connection. Telling a person to be grateful whenever they express disappointment can prevent important conversations from happening.

At the same time, unmet needs should not become a reason to dismiss every positive contribution. If someone is making a sincere effort, acknowledging that effort matters, even when there are still areas that need attention.

A healthy relationship makes room for both statements: “I appreciate what you do for me,” and “There are things I still need us to work on.”

Those statements do not contradict one another.

The Fear of Being the Only One Trying

When someone begins feeling that love is one sided, they may start protecting themselves by reducing their own effort.

They may stop initiating conversations, stop suggesting activities, or become less affectionate because they want to see whether the other person will notice. Sometimes they are hoping the other person will recognize the change and make an effort without being asked.

The intention may be to discover whether the relationship matters equally to both people. But the result can become another layer of distance.

The other person may not understand why the behavior has changed. They may interpret the withdrawal as disinterest or assume that the relationship is finally becoming less demanding.

Now both people are waiting.

One is waiting for evidence that the other cares enough to reach out. The other is waiting for an explanation of why the relationship suddenly feels different.

Neither is addressing the concern directly.

There is nothing wrong with recognizing when you have been carrying too much responsibility. You are allowed to establish boundaries, stop overextending yourself, and reconsider patterns that are affecting your well-being.

But testing someone through silence or withdrawal is not a reliable way to determine what they feel. If something is missing, it deserves to be discussed. If the other person repeatedly refuses to respond or make an effort after the concern has been communicated, that behavior provides important information too.

You should not have to exhaust yourself to prove that you deserve consideration.

When One Person Is Carrying the Emotional Weight

There are relationships in which one person becomes responsible for nearly every difficult conversation.

They are the ones who notice when something is wrong, bring up concerns, ask questions, attempt reconciliation, and try to understand what caused the disagreement. They may spend considerable time examining their own behavior while the other person rarely considers their contribution to the problem.

Eventually, the emotional responsibility becomes exhausting.

The person carrying that weight may begin questioning whether they are asking for too much. They may wonder whether their expectations are unreasonable or whether they should simply become more patient.

But patience cannot replace mutual effort.

A person can be understanding without accepting a permanent imbalance. They can recognize another person’s circumstances without becoming responsible for every emotional need in the relationship. They can offer support while still expecting their partner to participate in resolving problems.

Relationships require more than one person willing to examine what needs to change.

If someone consistently refuses to communicate, dismisses concerns, or expects the other person to repair every disagreement, the issue is no longer simply a difference in how love is expressed. It is a question of whether both people are willing to participate in the relationship with consideration and accountability.

Love cannot thrive when one person is continually expected to do the work of two.

The Difference Between Wanting More and Expecting Everything

There is also a point where expectations deserve examination.

Wanting attention, affection, honesty, and consideration is reasonable. Wanting to feel important to the person you love is not an unreasonable demand.

However, wanting more does not automatically mean that the other person is failing. Sometimes our expectations grow beyond what we have communicated. Sometimes we compare our relationship with what we see other people experiencing without knowing what happens behind their doors. Sometimes we expect another person to meet every emotional need because we have not developed other sources of support, identity, or personal fulfillment.

A partner can contribute greatly to our happiness, but they cannot become responsible for every part of it.

Healthy love allows two people to remain individuals while building a shared life. Each person must be able to express needs without making the other responsible for managing every feeling, fear, or disappointment.

There is a difference between asking someone to be considerate and expecting them to organize their entire life around your emotional security.

Recognizing that difference requires honesty from both sides.

One person may need to communicate their expectations more clearly. The other may need to become more attentive to the ways their actions affect the relationship. Both may need to recognize that love is not measured by how completely one person can satisfy the other, but by how consistently they are willing to consider one another.

When the Relationship Needs a Different Kind of Effort

Sometimes the solution is not for either person to do more of the same thing. It is for both to understand what kind of effort would actually make a difference.

A person who wants more meaningful time together may not need additional activities. They may need the time they already share to include fewer distractions and more genuine attention.

Someone who feels overwhelmed by repeated conversations may not need their partner to stop expressing concerns. They may need those conversations to happen in a calmer way, with enough room to listen and respond.

A person who wants reassurance may need consistency rather than constant promises. Someone who feels unappreciated may need acknowledgment that does not disappear as soon as the next disagreement occurs.

The important thing is to move beyond general statements about trying harder.

What does effort actually look like to each person? What changes would make the relationship healthier? Which responsibilities need to be shared more fairly? What can each person realistically commit to doing differently?

Those questions create a clearer path forward because they turn disappointment into something both people can examine.

Promises are important, but consistent behavior is what gives those promises meaning.

Looking Beneath the Surface

Consider whether you have begun measuring your relationship by everything you believe you give compared with everything you receive.

Are you recognizing the efforts your partner makes, even when they are different from what you would naturally do? Have you clearly communicated what you need, or have you been hoping they will eventually recognize it on their own?

If you feel that you are carrying too much, ask whether the imbalance is temporary because of difficult circumstances or whether it has become a repeated pattern that your partner is unwilling to address.

Also consider whether you are giving freely or whether you are giving with an expectation that the other person will respond in exactly the same way. It is reasonable to expect mutual consideration, but affection should not become a transaction in which every act must be repaid.

If you are the person receiving someone’s concern, ask whether you are listening to what they need or focusing entirely on everything you believe you already provide. Are you willing to recognize the difference between your intentions and their experience?

Neither person should have to prove their worth through endless sacrifice, and neither should expect the relationship to survive on affection without consistent consideration.

When Love Needs to Be Recognized

Sometimes love feels one sided because two people have stopped recognizing the ways they are trying to reach one another. At other times, it feels one sided because the effort truly has become unequal.

The difference matters.

If both people are making an effort but struggling to recognize it, honest communication may help them understand what each has been offering and what remains missing. If one person is repeatedly refusing to participate, the relationship may require deeper decisions about boundaries, expectations, and whether meaningful change is possible.

Understanding the other person’s perspective should never require abandoning your own.

You can appreciate someone’s effort while asking for more. You can recognize their limitations while acknowledging your disappointment. You can love someone without accepting a relationship in which your needs continually go unheard.

The same is true for the other person. They deserve to have their efforts recognized, their circumstances understood, and their own needs considered.

A healthy relationship does not require perfect equality in every moment. There will be seasons when one person needs more support, and there will be times when responsibilities are not distributed evenly. What matters is whether both people remain committed to consideration, communication, and shared responsibility over time.

Love should not become a competition over who has sacrificed the most.

It should be a relationship in which both people can recognize the effort being made, speak honestly about what is missing, and make meaningful changes when something is not working.

Because feeling loved is important, but so is knowing that the person beside you is willing to understand what love needs to look like in your shared life.

And sometimes, before deciding that someone is not trying, we need to understand what they believe they are giving. Just as importantly, before expecting someone to be satisfied with what we offer, we need to be willing to hear what they have been missing.

That is where the next part of the story begins: understanding the weight people carry when they feel that no matter how hard they try, the person they love still does not understand them.
