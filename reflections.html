Reader Reflections

A place to slow down, look honestly at what we feel and see, and consider what we may have missed.

A Few Questions to Carry With You

Understanding often begins with a better question.

Use these prompts privately, in a journal, or as a starting point for a thoughtful conversation. You do not have to answer every question at once.

01 · PERSPECTIVE

What might I not be seeing?

Think of a recent disagreement. What parts of the other person's experience might be missing from the version of events you keep replaying?

02 · COMMUNICATION

What have I left unsaid?

Is there a feeling, need, or concern you expect someone else to recognize without your having clearly expressed it?

03 · LISTENING

Am I listening to understand?

When someone explains how they feel, do you become curious about their experience, or begin preparing your defense before they finish?

04 · EXPECTATIONS

Have I made my expectations clear?

Consider whether the other person knows what you hope for, what matters to you, and what you need from the relationship.

05 · ACCOUNTABILITY

What part belongs to me?

Without taking responsibility for another person's choices, what words, reactions, or patterns of your own deserve an honest look?

06 · NEXT STEPS

What would understanding change?

If both perspectives were heard clearly, what might become easier to decide? What boundaries, conversations, or changes might still be necessary?

A Thought to Remember

Understanding another person's perspective does not require you to agree with everything they say, excuse hurtful behavior, or abandon your own needs. It simply asks you to see the situation as clearly and honestly as you can before deciding what comes next.
